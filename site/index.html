What is Jev, and why should I use it?

Name, one line about me. “You have probably heard of Jev. I spent some time building with it, and this is what I learned.”

Two ways to use AI

Chat

We ask.
It writes.

Decide

A program asks.
The model picks.

Most of us use AI by chatting: we ask, and the model writes. Jev works in a different way. A program asks it a question, and it picks an answer.

“Most of us use AI by chatting. We ask, it writes. Jev does not write. It decides.” The plan: how it works, two things I built with it, and when to use it.

A model for programs, not people

Chat model (LLM)Jev
What it doeswrites textpicks an answer
Who uses the resulta persona program

A chat model writes text for a person to read. Jev picks an answer for a program to use.

“The kind of decision an expert makes in one second.”

TypeSafe says most calls take about 100 milliseconds. You pay $0.042 for every million tokens of text you send, and nothing for the answers.

From my laptop I saw about 250 milliseconds for the full trip, and about 60 of those were inside TypeSafe's servers.

How one call works

your text
+ your questionseach with its own options
→
Jev
→
one answer per questionand how sure it is

You send some text and a few questions. Each question has a fixed list of possible answers. Jev gives back one answer for each question, and a number that says how sure it is.

The answer is always one of your options. It cannot invent a new one. You can ask many questions in one call, and they are answered at the same time.

Three kinds of question

Choice

Pick one option.

Which bin? → brown

Score

Pick a level.

How dirty is it? → 1.35 of 2

Noul

Yes or no, as a probability.

Is it packaging? → 0.94

Choice picks one option from your list. Score picks a level on a scale that you describe. Noul answers yes or no, as a probability.

The examples are real answers from the recycling demo: a wine cork, an oily napkin, a yogurt pot.

“I'm not sure” is useful

banana peel sure

used pen not sure

Sure → act.   Not sure → check, or ask a person.

Jev gives a probability for every option. When one option has almost all of it, Jev is sure. When it is spread over several options, Jev is not sure, and your program can ask a person.

The model tells you how sure it is, and your program can use that. High confidence does not mean the answer is correct. It means the model did not hesitate.

    When reading gets cheap
    
    
    One person, one minute for each proposal: about 600 hours.
    
    
    Jev: 20 minutes and $1.34.
    
    
    When something gets very cheap, people find new uses for it. That idea comes from the economist William Stanley Jevons, and it is where the name Jev comes from.
    
  
    
  
    Reading every proposal would take one person about 600 hours. Jev did it in 20 minutes for $1.34. When something gets very cheap, people find new uses for it: that is the idea behind the name Jev.
    
  
    Could one person read them all? Not really. When something gets very cheap, people find new uses for it.
    

    



    
  
    
    
    When to use it, and when not
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Good for
    
        
    • sorting and routing
    • checking and scoring
    • big piles of text
    • things that must be fast
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Not good for
    
        
    • writing text
    • maths and dates
    • text that tries to trick it
    • images
    
      
    
    
    
  
    
  
    Jev is good when a program needs a quick decision about some text. It is not good at writing, at maths or at dates, and it cannot look at images. It works together with chat models; it does not replace them.
    
  
    It works with LLMs, not instead of them. TypeSafe publishes this list of weak points themselves.
